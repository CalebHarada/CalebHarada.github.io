I am a guest on this &#699;&#257;ina visiting for the 
purpose of scientific research. 

I would like acknowledge that the &#699;&#257;ina on which I live and work, 
O&#699;ahu, is part of the larger territory recognized by Indigenous Hawaiians 
as their ancestral grandmother, Papah&#257;naumoku. 

I recognize that her majesty Queen Lili&#699;uokalani 
yielded the Hawaiian Kingdom and these territories under duress and protest to 
the United States to avoid the bloodshed of her people. I further recognize 
that Hawai&#699;i remains an illegally occupied state of America.

I also recognize and acknowledge the significant cultural role and 
reverence that Maunakea has within the Native Hawaiian community.
The astronomical community is most fortunate to have the opportunity to 
conduct observations from this sacred mountaintop.

I recognize that each moment I am in Hawai&#699;i she nourishes and gifts me 
with the opportunity to breathe her air, eat from her soils, drink from her 
waters, bathe in her sun, swim in her oceans, be kissed by her rains, and be 
embraced by her winds. I further recognize that generations of Indigenous 
Hawaiians and their knowledge systems shaped Hawai&#699;i in sustainable ways 
that allow me to enjoy these gifts today. For this I am grateful and as a guest,
I seek to support the varied strategies that the Indigenous peoples of 
Hawai&#699;i are using to protect their land and their communities, and I 
commit to dedicating time and resources to working in solidarity.
