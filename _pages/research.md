At a Glance: Worlds Around Other Stars
--------------------------------------

{% include toc %}

As the remnants of star formation, planets help tell the story of how solar 
systems like ours form and evolve over their lifetimes. Observations of planets 
both in our solar system and beyond demonstrate an incredible diversity of 
possible planet formation outcomes in terms of mass, radius, temperature, age, 
orbital characteristics, atmospheric and interior compositions and structures, 
host star properties, system architectures, and cosmic birth environments. And 
of course, planets are the only objects known so far to be capable of supporting 
life. However, the census of exoplanets is still largely incomplete, and 
countless questions about our cosmic origins remain unanswered. Just how 
special is our solar system? How did our planet come to be? 
Are we alone?

Astrophysical Habitability
--------------------------


Despite thousands of exoplanet discoveries, no unambiguously habitable exoplanets have been
detected and their formation processes are not fully understood. Current assumptions regarding
the habitability of exoplanets are largely based on our understanding of life on Earth. Namely,
the consensus that carbon-based life on Earth requires water has motivated searches for habitable
planets to focus on identifying environments that can sustain stable liquid water over long (∼Gyr)
timescales. The presence of liquid water would also imply atmospheric pressures and temperatures
that are suitable for biological reactions to occur. As a result, the circumstellar habitable zone (HZ)
has long been defined as the range of star&ndash;planet distances where conditions are neither too hot nor
too cold to allow stable liquid water to exist on a rocky planet's surface.

However, the boundaries of the HZ are not well-defined. Dozens (likely more) of higher-order
astrophysical effects determine whether any individual planet could be habitable. In no particular 
order and not an exhaustive list, these include planetary atmospheric composition and circulation, 
cloud cover, climate feedback (e.g., the carbonate&ndash;silicate cycle), volcanic outgassing, 
tidal or radiogenic heating, orbital eccentricity and obliquity, and land/ocean fraction. 
[Meadows and Barnes (2018)](https://scixplorer.org/abs/2018haex.bookE..57M/abstract) provide a nice
review of possible factors affecting habitability ranging from stellar effects
(e.g., spectral energy distribution, composition and structure, galactic location) to system-wide
effects (e.g., sibling planets, orbital evolution) to planetary effects (e.g., interior, 
atmosphere, orbital dynamics, surface conditions).
